## 📈 Measuring Whether A Change Helped

Most tuning changes are made on feel and never verified. That is how
configurations drift.

**What to measure**

| **Change** | **Measure** |
| --- | --- |
| Overlay layers | frame time, not FPS |
| Assist tuning | hit rate over a fixed number of engagements |
| Cosmetic layer | nothing — it is visual only |
| Module count | frame time again |

**Why FPS is the wrong number**

FPS is an average over a window. A configuration that drops one frame every
fifty shows almost no FPS change and feels visibly worse. Frame time shows the
spike directly.

**The fixed-sample method**

Change one value, play ten engagements, count outcomes. Change it back, play ten
more. Ten is not statistically rigorous, but it is enough to notice a large
difference and it takes twenty minutes instead of a week of vague impressions.

**The trap of compounding changes**

Three changes at once and you cannot attribute the result. One at a time is
slower per change and far faster overall.

**Keeping a note**

One line per change: what you changed, what you measured, what happened. After a
month that note is the only reliable record of why the config looks the way it
does.
